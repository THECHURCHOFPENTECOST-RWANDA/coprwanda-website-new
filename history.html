Our History

The history of The Church of Pentecost International Rwanda (COPIRWA) is a testimony of faith, missionary commitment, perseverance and God's faithfulness. From humble beginnings, the church has grown into a nationwide Pentecostal movement.

1. Pre-Founding Era and Divine Connections (1997–1999)

In 1997, in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, Ghanaian missionary Pastor Samuel Yaw Antwi met Brother Kalambi Foustin, a Congolese national living in Burundi. Following their missionary work, Brother Foustin was prayed for and commissioned to pioneer The Church of Pentecost in Burundi and coordinate expansion across the Great Lakes region.

In 1999, during a visit to Burundi, Pastor Samuel Yaw Antwi learned from Brother Foustin about Brother Jean-Marie Wedundu, a Congolese national living in Rwanda who had started a home cell group and was preparing for the establishment of the church in Rwanda.

2. Official Arrival and Early Growth (2000–2005)

On 12 August 2000, Pastor Samuel Yaw Antwi and Brother Kalambi Foustin arrived in Rwanda. They were welcomed by Brother Jean-Marie Wedundu and six foundational members of a 36-member home cell group.

In late 2000, Brother Jean-Marie Wedundu was introduced as the leader of the new church. The congregation initially met in rented premises in Kacyiru, Kigali, and grew to 222 members.

In 2001, Apostle Sidiki Traore visited and ministered to the young church. Between 2001 and 2005, the main base moved to Nyabugogo, with financial and structural support from COP USA branches in Charlotte, Boston and Bronx.

By 31 December 2005, the church had 318 members across six assemblies. Chairman Apostle Dr. Michael Ntumy visited Rwanda and officially introduced Pastor Jean-Marie Wedundu as the first National Head. During this visit, 25 Elders, 22 Deacons and 23 Deaconesses were ordained.

3. Expansion and Institutional Challenges (2006–2010)

In 2006, International Missions Director Apostle Dr. S. K. Baidoo visited Rwanda to ordain leaders. Assemblies were opened in Gisenyi, Cyangugu and Gitarama. Butare Assembly followed in 2007.

In 2008, the national headquarters and main Kigali church moved from Nyabugogo to Muhima. Additional assemblies were opened in Rusumo and Ruhengeri. In 2009, assemblies were also opened in Nyamata and Gikondo.

During 2009–2010, the church faced serious institutional and financial challenges. Most assemblies closed, leaving only two active assemblies: Kabuga and Muhari. Pastor Jean-Marie Wedundu was transferred to Uganda.

4. Revival, Reconstruction and Legal Recognition (2010–2015)

In November 2010, Pastor Peter Kofi Dzemekey, now Apostle, was sent to Rwanda as National Head and missionary in charge. He inherited a church with only seven members and began a revival and reconstruction effort.

In 2012, with support from the International Missions Office in Accra, the church purchased a permanent national mission house in Remera, Kigali, and a ministry vehicle. It also secured official legal recognition authorising the church to operate and establish branches throughout Rwanda.

In the same year, Chairman Apostle Dr. Opoku Onyinah visited to dedicate the Remera mission house and ordain five Elders, ten Deacons and twenty Deaconesses.

In 2014, Pastor Dzemekey was ordained an Apostle. Three Rwandan leaders—Twagiramungu Felician, Onesmus Nweni and Bimenyimana Xavier—were called into ministry as probationary overseers.

On 8 February 2015, International Missions Director Apostle Emmanuel Gyesi-Addo and his wife visited to dedicate the newly purchased and renovated Central Church building. Apostle Dzemekey completed his five-year tenure and transferred to Ghana on 23 August 2015.

5. Institutional Consolidation (2015)

In August 2015, Pastor Francis Agyemang Badu arrived from Ghana as the new National Head. The welcome and farewell service was officiated by pioneer Apostle Samuel Yaw Antwi, together with regional heads from DR Congo, Burundi and Uganda.

By December 2015, the church had grown to 879 members, 11 assemblies and three pastoral districts. The membership was overwhelmingly made up of indigenous Rwandans.

6. Strategic Merger and Indigenous Pastoral Ministry (2018–2019)

In 2018, The Church of Pentecost Rwanda merged with the local church CEPEA. This merger strengthened the church's unity, visibility, structural reach and presence in major sectors of society.

In 2019, the first full pastors from the indigenous Rwandan ministry were ordained: Pastor Twagiramungu Felician and Pastor Ngwabije Sylvestre.

7. Modern Era and National Growth (As of December 2025)

By the end of December 2025, The Church of Pentecost International Rwanda had developed into a nationwide church structured into five Administrative Areas and 21 Pastoral Districts, with approximately 80 active assemblies and approximately 14,000 believers.

From its beginnings in 2000, COPIRWA continues to pursue its mission of proclaiming the Gospel, making disciples and transforming communities through the power of the Holy Spirit.
